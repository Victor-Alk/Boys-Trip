# D18 — Guatemala: Volcanoes and the Lost Cities of the Maya

Research notes, checked 27 November 2026. Data file: `data/destinations/D18-guatemala.json`.

## Overview

A 13-day route (12–24 February 2027): Antigua (3 nights), an Acatenango overnight facing Fuego (conditional), Lake Atitlán (2), Chichicastenango and Quetzaltenango (2), a domestic flight to Flores, Tikal (camping) and Yaxhá, and an airport night. No self-drive: private vans, public lanchas and TAG/Avianca flights.

## Screening verdict

- **Seasonality:** February is dry and cool in the highlands, hot in the Petén; best month. March is workable until Semana Santa (21–28 March 2027).
- **Flights:** Paris–Guatemala City is the main cost; no live fare was available (estimate €920 realistic).
- **Local costs:** low for food and local transport; mostly estimates (see workings).
- **Security:** eligible on the redesigned route; high ordinary crime; one unresolved access restriction (Acatenango/Fuego).
- **Cultural interest:** very high (Maya markets, cooperatives, Tikal, colonial Antigua).
- **Adventure:** Acatenango, Santa María, Tikal camping.
- **Independent-travel feasibility:** guided and hired-driver; self-drive possible legally but not advised.

## Itinerary rationale

Highlands first (altitude gain gradual: Antigua 1,500 m, Acatenango 3,500 m, Atitlán 1,562 m, Xela 2,330 m), then a flight to avoid a long overland journey to the Petén. Chichicastenango on Thursday 18 Feb, a market day. Santa María and Zunil give a second volcano and hot springs and replace Acatenango if it is closed. Semuc Champey (8–10 h from Antigua or Flores by shuttle, Q50 entry, per Wikivoyage) does not fit; see alternatives.

## Transport and driving legality

- Domestic flights run only GUA–Flores (TAG, Avianca) (Wikivoyage).
- FCDO: avoid public buses (gang attacks, accidents); armed attacks and carjacking on tourist routes; UK photocard licence valid up to 3 months; hire firms may require an IDP and experience. Other licence rules (a commonly cited 30 days) not verified.
- Motorcycles: nobody without the correct licence; use tuk-tuks with drivers, boats, vans.
- Netherlands: in the Petén use a licensed transport company; Tikal by plane or organised transport; arrange police escort for groups.

## Security assessment (checked 27 Nov 2026)

- **UK FCDO** (updated 16 Nov 2026): all-but-essential only within 5 km of the Mexican border (Pacific coast to Gracias a Dios) and Santa Ana Huista, San Antonio Huista, La Democracia (Huehuetenango). 30-day state of emergency in Zacapa and Izabal. No restriction listed for Petén, Escuintla or San Marcos. Safety page: high violent crime, express kidnapping, armed attacks on tourist routes, no recent terrorism.
- **Netherlands Worldwide** (18 Nov 2026): yellow; Izabal/Zacapa emergency; Petén by licensed transport, Tikal by plane or organised transport; border smuggling.
- **France Diplomatie** (16 Nov 2026): page lists the Izabal/Zacapa state of siege, Fuego red alert (4 Aug 2026), armed attacks in Guatemala City zones 6, 7, 9, 12, 13, 14, 16, 25, RN14 closure, and "ascents of Fuego and Acatenango prohibited until further notice". The colour level is a map image and could not be read; the text names no zone déconseillée for the route.
- **US State Department** (403) and **Smartraveller** (timeout): could not be fetched; not used.
- **Fuego:** an August 2026 eruption triggered red alert in Sacatepéquez, Escuintla and Chimaltenango (Al Jazeera, 5 Aug). INGUAT said tourism was normal on 7 Aug while the Acatenango/Fuego ascent stayed restricted (Revista EyN); one other report said Acatenango reopened on 7 Aug. Current status unconfirmed; the web search budget ran out before it could be checked further.

Classification: **eligible** — route avoids all against-all-but-essential areas, the state of siege and El Mirador/Laguna del Tigre; conflict low, terrorism low, kidnapping moderate (express kidnapping). Acatenango has a fallback. A21 may prefer "conditional" if the ban persists.

## Alternatives considered

- **El Mirador (5-day mule/walk via Carmelita):** dropped. Driest March–April (Wikivoyage); needs ≈ 5–6 days and a guide; sits near the Mexican border in the Laguna del Tigre/Mirador area, which the user flagged; route-specific advice, costs and seasonal access could not be verified. A 15-day variant exists but would exceed €2,500.
- **Semuc Champey/Lanquín:** swap Xela for Cobán and Semuc (about 8–10 h shuttle legs); FCDO notes frequent protests near Semuc Champey.
- **Tajumulco/San Marcos:** avoided (rural high-crime region).
- **Pacaya:** in Escuintla, within the Fuego red-alert area in August; not used.
- **Cheaper variant:** economical scenario €1,778 with shared shuttles and camping.
