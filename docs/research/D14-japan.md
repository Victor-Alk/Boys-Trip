## Overview

"Japan — Kyushu, Calderas, Hot Springs and Hidden Shrines": a 14-day loop, recommended window 2–15 March 2027. Days 3–9 by rental car (Hakata to Kagoshima, one-way), then Shinkansen, ferry, bus and JR rail. ## Itinerary rationale

Volcano ladder: Beppu hells (day 3), Aso caldera (6), Kirishima (8), Sakurajima (9), Unzen (11). Culture in between: Kunisaki temples (4), Takachiho kagura (7), Kumamoto (10), Nagasaki (12), Arita (13). Daily driving 1–3 h. Flexible days: 4, 6, 12. Car returned in Kagoshima to avoid backtracking and a long return drive.

Not included: **Yakushima** (Jomon Sugi). Ministry of the Environment page and operator guides: roads to trail entrances close in heavy rain or snow; the Arakawa trailhead is closed to private cars between 1 March and 30 November with a dedicated bus (bus tickets bought in advance); the hike is a full day. Adding two days plus a ferry from Kagoshima (about 2 h to Miyanoura per one source) is feasible in principle in Feb/March but snow can cancel it. Left as an optional extension, not planned. Not researched: Kunisaki campgrounds; Shimabara details.

## Transport and driving legality

- **IDP:** Japan accepts a 1949 Geneva Convention IDP (JAF; UK FCDO). 1968 Vienna-format permits are not accepted (commonly stated; confirm). A foreign licence is valid in Japan for one year from entry.
- **JAF translation instead of an IDP:** licences from Switzerland, Germany, France, Belgium, Monaco and Taiwan (JAF page); translations only from JAF or the issuing country's embassy/consulate in Japan. One university page also lists Estonia; Slovenia was not confirmed by any source found. Check JAF before booking; get it before departure where possible. This is conditional on licence country; the research does not assume nationalities.
- **Drivers:** two of four hold car licences; both must be named on the rental contract. The other two do not ride scooters or motorcycles. Alternatives: rail, bus, ferry, taxi, walking.
- **Tolls:** ETC card supplied by the rental firm. Kyushu Expressway Pass (KEP): foreign passport holders renting in Kyushu, 2–10 day plans; official site says sales end 31 March 2027; prices in sources conflict (older tables 2 days 6,200 JPY up to newer figures such as 15,000 JPY for 6 days), and this route is mostly local roads, so the pass probably does not pay back. A ToCoo! Expressway Pass was mentioned by one guide (not verified).
- **JR Kyushu Rail Pass:** alternative for a car-free variant; price and coverage not checked.
- **Winter:** ask for studless tyres or chains; Aso, Takachiho and Ebino roads can ice or close.

## Cheaper variant

An 11-day rail-and-bus Kyushu loop (Fukuoka, Beppu, Kurokawa/Aso by bus, Kumamoto, Nagasaki, Arita) without a rental car: remove vehicle, fuel, tolls, insurance (about 1,200 EUR per vehicle, about 170 EUR per person with contingency), remove 3 rural nights (about 180 EUR) and 3 days of food (about 66 EUR). That leaves a rough total near 2,000 EUR if flights stay near 960, i.e. at the boundary of the preferred band. Not costed line by line; the Aso/Takachiho/Kirishima legs are poorly served by public transport, so this changes the concept. A flight-only saving (a one-stop flight booked far in advance) is the main lever.
