# D05 — Algeria: Roman Shores and the Tassili Galleries

Research notes by agent A05. Verification date: 2026-11-27. Data file: `data/destinations/D05-algeria.json`.

## Overview

Concept as briefed: Roman archaeological heritage plus a Saharan expedition. The redesigned 13-day plan is Algiers (Casbah), Tipasa and Cherchell, Constantine and Tiddis, then a night flight to Djanet and a five-day Tassili n'Ajjer trek with a licensed Tuareg agency, finishing with a dune camp on the Erg Admer.

**What changed from the original concept.** The classic Roman circuit of Timgad and Djémila was dropped: France Diplomatie lists the Batna wilaya (Timgad) and the Sétif wilaya (Djémila) as orange ("déconseillées sauf raison impérative") in its carte de vigilance (zone map updated 2 March 2026, page dated 15 September 2026). The Red Tadrart (Tadrart Rouge, Tin Merzouga) and Oued Essendilene were also dropped: on an approximate calculation using Natural Earth borders they lie about 65–150 km from the Niger border (inside the 200 km strip used by the Netherlands and Canada) or within roughly 10–70 km of Libya.

## February vs March

- Djanet NOAA 1991–2020 normals (via Wikipedia): February max 22.6 °C, min 6.3 °C (record low −3.6 °C), rain 0.8 mm; March max 27.2 °C, min 11.5 °C (record low 0.8 °C), rain 2.1 mm. The Tassili plateau (about 1,500–1,900 m) is several degrees colder (planning estimate, no station data).
- Algiers coast: roughly 15–16 °C by day in February and 17–18 °C in March with rain on 8–10 days a month (approximate regional norms, not sourced to a station page).
- Wind and dust: sirocco-type and spring dust events are more frequent from March (general pattern; no 2027 forecast). A sandstorm can delay the night flights.
- Ramadan 2027 about 8 Feb – 9 Mar; Eid al-Fitr about 9–10 Mar. A trip starting 14 March avoids both; Eid holiday demand pushes domestic flights up around the 9th–12th.
- Lunar New Year: not relevant.

## Itinerary rationale

North (days 1–5): Algiers for the UNESCO Casbah, then the Tipaza coast with a driver (Tipasa and Cherchell), a Constantine hop by air and Tiddis. These sites are not in a zone listed by France, FCDO or the Netherlands. Belgian advice prefers flying between northern cities, so the plan flies rather than driving long road legs. Day 6 is the contingency buffer. South (days 7–12): Djanet briefing, ascent to the Tassili plateau, the Tamrit cypress sector, painted shelters at Sefar and Jabbaren, descent to Erg Admer and a night flight back to Algiers on day 12. The Tassili part draws on the structure of operator programmes (for example a Nomade Aventure 8-day Tassili trek with six bivouacs) but is shortened to five days.

Limitations: plateau and dune-camp coordinates in the route data are schematic, not surveyed. Jabbaren is about 51 km from the Libyan border using a position converted from a published UTM reference for an arch there; Tamrit is described only as "northeast of Djanet" and could lie within 50 km of Libya. The agency must supply its GPS route.

## Transport and driving legality

- International: Paris CDG–Algiers about 2 h 25 min. Air Algérie operates from CDG (fare not verified); Transavia from Orly (comparison-site offers €113–129 return for Feb/Mar 2027).
- Domestic: Algiers–Djanet is served by Air Algérie's subsidiary Domestic Airlines since 6 February 2026, 7–8 weekly flights reported, usually night departures, about 2 h 15 min. Return fares about US$115–150 booked six weeks ahead (trade press).
- Self-drive: not planned. Algeria allows foreign drivers in the north (Dutch licence stated valid; IDP rule not verified), but road safety is poor and checkpoints frequent. The France Diplomatie text states that travel without a guide in the Algerian Sahara is not possible.
- Motorcycles: none. Two of four hold car licences; none ride unlicensed.

## Security assessment (checked 2026-11-27)

**France Diplomatie** (page dated 15 Sept 2026, zone map 2 March 2026): red for land-border zones (Libya, Mali, Niger, Morocco; the Tunisian border from and south of Tébessa), orange for Aïn Defla, Batna and Sétif wilayas, Aurès, Chréa, Tindouf and Hassi Messaoud, yellow ("vigilance renforcée") for the Sahara around Tamanrasset, green for Algiers and Oran. Illizi and Djanet are not named; the Sahara text requires an accredited agency, a guide, authorisation and army-monitored routes with escort. Border strip width is not given. The web page itself did not expose the map; the zone text was read from the ministry's printable PDF.

**UK FCDO** (updated 25 Sept 2026): against all travel within 30 km of the Libya, Mauritania, Mali and Niger borders and the Tunisia border strips; terrorists likely to attempt attacks, higher threat in the Sahara and border areas; kidnapping risk in remote and border desert areas. Djanet, Tassili, Illizi not named.

**Netherlands** (30 July 2026): yellow elsewhere, red for 50 km along the Libya border and 200 km along Mauritania, Mali and Niger borders; Tamanrasset and Djanet named among places with terrorism and kidnapping risk; travel with an approved guide.

**Canada** (9 Sept 2026): avoid all travel within 200 km of Mali, Mauritania and Niger borders and 50 km of Libya.

**US State Department**: Level 2 overall (25 Aug 2026), with Sahara overland travel outside an organised group at Level 4, per a search summary. The page returned HTTP 403 and could not be read directly, so this is second-hand.

**Belgium** (12 Nov 2026): classic Grand Sud circuits (Tamanrasset, Djanet) dangerous due to persistent terrorism risk; mentions the 2024 killing in Djanet and a January 2025 hostage case near Tamanrasset.

**Smartraveller**: fetch timed out; no Australian level is recorded.

**Incidents**: a Swiss tourist was killed in a cafe in Djanet on 11 October 2024 (reported by Swiss media and wire services; motive unclear). A Spanish tourist was kidnapped on 15 January 2025 near Tamanrasset and taken to Mali (reported by Spanish media; release details single-source).

**Border distances** (approximate, Natural Earth 1:10m country borders, straight-line, computed in this project): Djanet town 64 km to Libya, 234 km to Niger; Djanet airport about 85 km to Libya, 211 km to Niger; Jabbaren (derived position) about 51 km to Libya; Tamrit sector (northeast guess) 29–53 km to Libya. Tadrart Rouge and Tin Merzouga are inside the 200 km Niger strip. Margins on the Djanet side are tens of kilometres, comparable to the uncertainty of the data and of the advisory wording.

**Classification: conditional.** No source lists Djanet or the Tassili in an orange or red zone, which would otherwise make the destination excluded. But official wording is unresolved on border strips versus the circuit's location, the Sahara is escort-only, and the 2024 and 2025 incidents show a live threat. If the operator's GPS route is within 50 km of Libya or 200 km of Niger, or if an advisory changes for Illizi or Djanet, the classification becomes excluded. A stricter reading of "significant terrorism or kidnapping risk" could also exclude it; this is flagged for A21.

## Alternatives considered

- **Original Roman circuit with Timgad and Djémila**: rejected because France lists Batna and Sétif wilayas orange.
- **Tadrart Rouge expedition (the classic "red Sahara")**: rejected, inside border strips as described above.
- **Ghardaïa and the M'zab valley** as the Saharan leg: about 600 km from Libya, in no orange or red zone named by France; reachable by domestic flight; lower walking-trek value and different concept (mud-brick cities, ibadite culture). Likely cheaper because the agency programme is shorter, but not costed.
- **Tunisia (D08)** offers Roman and desert-camp features with an easier security profile.
