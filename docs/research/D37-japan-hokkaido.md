# D37 — Japan — Hokkaido, Drift Ice and Cranes

Researched 2026-11-28 (participant proposal). All prices are planning estimates unless stated; no live fare or booking quote was seen on a booking page.

## Overview
A 13-day winter loop through eastern Hokkaido, 12–24 February 2027: Sapporo and Upopoy (Ainu museum), a flight to Memanbetsu, a 4WD rental car through Abashiri (icebreaker), Utoro (drift-ice walk), Rausu (Steller's sea eagles), Kawayu/Lake Kussharo, Lake Mashū, Lake Akan and Akan Ainu Kotan, Tsurui (red-crowned cranes) and Kushiro, then Kushiro to Haneda and home. Distinct from D14 (Kyushu: volcanoes, onsen, historic towns in the warm south): this is a sea-ice and wildlife trip in deep winter.

## Screening verdict
- **Seasonality:** drift ice arrives about late January and leaves by early April; second half of February is typically best (S10). Rausu cruises run late January to early March.
- **Flights:** no direct Paris–Sapporo flights found. Snapshots: ANA from EUR 1,045 (2–11 Feb) and 1,089 (25 Feb–20 Mar), Cathay Pacific EUR 1,296 (S16, S17). Read from search-result snippets, not booking pages.
- **Local costs:** high (Japan, winter, one-way rental, two domestic flights).
- **Security:** eligible; natural hazards only.
- **Cultural interest:** Ainu culture at Upopoy and Akan Kotan.
- **Adventure:** guided drift-ice walk, snowshoeing; no technical skills.
- **Independent-travel feasibility:** good but winter driving is the main risk.
- **Budget verdict:** realistic EUR 3,191 per person, outside the EUR 2,500 limit. The economical scenario (EUR 2,351) is the credible cheaper variant.

## February vs March
February (preferred): ice at its peak; Rausu cruises run; Sounkyo ice festival (23 Jan–7 Mar 2027, S15); very cold (night -10 to -25 C inland). March: ice retreating, Rausu cruises usually end early March, Upopoy closed 27 Feb–8 Mar (S13), milder, cranes still fed to late March (S12). The Sapporo Snow Festival is 4–11 February 2027 (S08, S09): it ends before the window, which avoids its hotel prices; a festival-start variant (depart ~2 Feb) would add Sapporo and Otaru but gives earlier, less reliable ice and higher prices. Lunar New Year 6 February brings regional visitors; Ramadan not relevant.

## Itinerary rationale
Two Sapporo nights include Upopoy (open Sundays; closed Mondays), then a domestic flight avoids a 5–6 h winter drive east. Eight nights east, ending at Kushiro so the loop is one-way (rental drop at Kushiro Airport). Shiretoko Pass (Route 334) is closed in winter (S14) so Utoro to Rausu is a long detour (estimated 3–4 h, 170 km; not verified). Day 11 is a flexible buffer. Day 12 flies Kushiro–Haneda with a hotel night; day 13 flies home. Camping: none.

## Transport and driving legality
Japan accepts only 1949 Geneva IDPs. Licences from France, Germany, Switzerland, Belgium, Monaco and Taiwan are not covered by an IDP: they need a Japanese translation, for French licences from JAF, online, about 2–3 business days, valid one year (S07). The brief says nobody may drive without the legal licence; two of the four drive. Winter rentals have studless tyres included (S20). One-way fee estimates: 5,400–10,000 JPY elsewhere in Hokkaido; Memanbetsu–Kushiro not listed, so EUR 150 is assumed. Alternatives: JR Senmo Line and buses (slow, infrequent in winter), taxis, or a driver-guide on days 5–7.

## Signature experiences
Drift-ice walk at Utoro (~8,500 JPY); Rausu eagle cruise (from ~5,000 JPY); cranes at Tsurui (free per a directory listing, unconfirmed); Ainu culture at Upopoy and Akan Kotan.

## Alternatives considered
- Sapporo Snow Festival start (4–11 Feb) and Otaru: dearer, earlier ice.
- Sounkyo Ice Fall Festival (23 Jan–7 Mar), Biei/Furano, Daisetsuzan: add 2 days and a drive; not included.
- Niseko or backcountry: not recommended (avalanche deaths).
- Self-drive replaced by rail and guided transfers: less flexible, costs similar.
