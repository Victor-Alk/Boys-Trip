# D23 — United States: The Winter Southern Crossing (New York to Los Angeles)

Researched 2026-11-28. Origin: participant proposal (2026-11-28). Status: security **eligible**; budget **outside the 2,500 EUR ceiling** (realistic about 3,430 EUR per person incl. flights), so not eligible for the final ranking.

## Overview

A one-way, two-driver road trip from New York JFK to Los Angeles LAX in 15 days (open-jaw flights from Paris CDG), on a southern line chosen to avoid the snow-prone northern routes: Washington, Shenandoah Skyline Drive, Knoxville, Nashville, Memphis, Oklahoma City, Amarillo, Tucumcari, Santa Fe, Petrified Forest/Holbrook, Grand Canyon South Rim, Joshua Tree, Los Angeles. About 5,100 km of driving over 12 driving days.

## February vs March

February: cheaper and quieter, but colder (Grand Canyon rim nights about -10 C), more ice on the Blue Ridge and northern Arizona/New Mexico, camping not realistic. Mardi Gras (9 February 2027) only matters if New Orleans is added (not recommended: crowds and prices). March: highs 9-24 C along the route, more daylight (daylight saving begins 14 March), but spring-break crowds at the Grand Canyon, Joshua Tree and Santa Monica, and early tornado season from Memphis to Oklahoma. Both months fall in a forecast El Nino winter: California declared a state of emergency on 21 September 2026 (France Diplomatie), which raises the chance of heavy rain and high-elevation snow in the Southwest. Climate figures in the data are approximate planning ranges, not downloaded station normals.

A coast-to-coast drive is 4,500 km on a direct line; the southern winter-safe line with the cultural stops used here is about 5,100 km. With 12 driving days that is 430 km/day on average, but it is not evenly spread: four days are 6 h or longer (Staunton-Knoxville 6 h, Memphis-Oklahoma City 7 h 30, Oklahoma City-Tucumcari 6 h 15, Grand Canyon-Joshua Tree 7 h). ## Transport and driving legality

- One vehicle, two licensed drivers (the two licence holders). A full-size SUV or minivan is needed for four adults and bags.
- One-way fees: Hertz's published sheet shows no fee between some California/Nevada airports but fees vary by location pair; Thrifty states a drop-off or mileage charge on top of the rate. Coast-to-coast quotes were not obtained; the 350/550/900 EUR figures are estimates. Some brands cap rental duration.
- Age: 25+ normally avoids young-renter fees; the group is 30-40.
- Licences: a Latin-script licence is generally accepted; the FCDO says an IDP (1949 convention) may also be needed and requirements vary by state and by rental company. Ask the rental company in writing; conditional on licence country.
- Insurance: US rentals include only the state-minimum liability; supplemental liability and a collision waiver are budgeted, partly via credit-card cover (check the card terms for rentals outside the home country).
- Motorcycles: none planned; nobody rides without a licence.

## Security assessment (checked 2026-11-28)

- France Diplomatie "États-Unis" page (updated 29 Oct 2026): no red/orange zone; the country is described as among the safer ones; city crime guidance (Washington, Memphis area, Los Angeles); tornado season from March; California declared an El Nino emergency on 21 Sep 2026; new CDC attestation (no stay in the DR Congo in the 21 days before boarding) for international flights to the US.
- UK FCDO (updated 20 Nov 2026): no advise-against warnings; terrorists "very likely" to attempt attacks in the US; violent crime and mass shootings rarely affect tourists; ESTA strictly enforced; IDP note; hire-car and weather guidance.
- Not retrieved: Netherlands Worldwide (URL returned 404), Smartraveller (connection timed out), US State Department (HTTP 403). The assessment rests on two authorities; re-check the others before booking.
- Classification: eligible. The terrorism threat is real but comparable to other Western countries and does not fall under the exclusion threshold; no conflict or kidnapping exposure. Reassess before booking and two weeks before departure.

## Alternatives considered

- **Shorter, cheaper western loop (credible cheaper variant):** fly CDG-LAX and back (about 650 EUR, estimate), drive a 12-day loop Los Angeles - Joshua Tree - Grand Canyon - Monument Valley/Zion area - Las Vegas - Death Valley - Los Angeles (about 2,800 km, no one-way fee). Rough estimate: car 12 x 72 + insurance 300 + fuel 250 = 1,414 group, lodging 20 room-nights x 100 = 2,000 group, so group 3,700 / 4 = 925 pp; individual (food 12 x 45, pass, others) about 800; local about 1,725 plus 12% = 1,930; plus flights 650 = about 2,580 EUR. Still near the ceiling; not verified.
- Fly Nashville to Albuquerque (about 150-250 EUR each) and skip 2,100 km and two heavy days; saves roughly 250 EUR of fuel and a night or two of lodging for the group, but loses the Plains crossing. Not priced precisely.
- I-70/I-80 northern routes: rejected for winter closures.
- Adding New Orleans: rejected; 2 extra days and the Mardi Gras premium in February.
