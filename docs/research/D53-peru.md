# D53 — Peru: Down the Colca, Across Titicaca, Up to Machu Picchu

Researched 2026-11-28 (participant proposal, 28 November 2026). Data: `data/destinations/D53-peru.json`.

## Overview

Fifteen days (5–19 March 2027): Lima (food) → Arequipa (2,335 m) → Chivay (3,650 m, over a ~4,900 m pass) → Cruz del Cóndor and a night at the Sangalle oasis in the Colca Canyon → Puno and Lake Titicaca (3,800 m; Uros, Amantaní homestay, Taquile) → Cusco (3,400 m) → Moray, Maras, Ollantaytambo → train to Aguas Calientes and Machu Picchu → fly Cusco–Lima–Paris. All by flight, bus, boat, rail and one hired-van day. No self-drive. Two flexible days (10 and 13) absorb rain, strikes and train disruption.

The route is ordered for acclimatisation: sea level, then 2,335 m, then 3,650 m (two nights), a canyon floor at ~2,100 m, 3,800 m, 3,400 m, and only then Ollantaytambo (2,790 m) and Machu Picchu (~2,430 m).

## February vs March

- **February:** wettest month in Cusco and Puno; highest landslide and train-closure risk; Inca Trail network closed for the entire month (2026 notice: Routes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 closed through 28 February, reopening 1 March — S09; the 2027 dates are not yet published); Carnival (~6–10 Feb 2027) and Puno's Candelaria festivities. Rating: poor.
- **March:** rains easing in the second half; network reopens 1 March (pattern); permits for early March sell out first because February travellers rebook. Semana Santa 22–28 March 2027 (Easter Sunday 28 March) avoided by ending on 19 March. Rating: fair.
- **El Niño 2026–27:** ENFEN/SENAMHI outlooks (Gestión, 31 Oct 2026, citing ENFEN) expect above-normal rain on the north and central coast and drier-than-normal conditions in the south-eastern sierra, with a marked deficit around Lake Titicaca. Lima and the north coast (not on the route apart from Lima) carry the flood risk. Forecasts can change; Cusco was not named in the article.
- Ramadan 2027 and Lunar New Year: not relevant.

## Itinerary rationale

1. **Lima first** for food, and to avoid arriving at altitude straight off a long-haul flight.
2. **Arequipa and Colca** next: gentle gain to 2,335 m, then a pass at ~4,900 m but sleeping at 3,650 m; the oasis night at ~2,100 m is physiologically a recovery night.
3. **Puno/Titicaca** at 3,800 m after four nights at altitude.
4. **Cusco** after the lake; day 10 is a slow day.
5. **Sacred Valley** (2,800–3,500 m) and **Machu Picchu** (~2,430 m) last, so the group is acclimatised.

Alternatives rejected: Nazca overflight (Aerodiana crash 1 Aug 2026, 13 dead; S13) and Paracas/Huacachina/Nazca (adds 2–3 days and a north-south coast drive for little). Rainbow Mountain/Palccoyo (4,900–5,000 m day trips, bad in rain) and Tambopata (extra flight and a lowland rain/mosquito season) are possible swaps for the spare days; they are not in the base plan.

## Inca Trail, Salkantay and Lares

- Classic Inca Trail: closed in February; 500 permits/day including staff (about 180–200 for trekkers); 2027 release dates not announced on the operator page checked (S11); listed from about USD 756 for 4 days with an operator. Check March 2027 availability as soon as permits are released. If obtained, it replaces days 11–13 and changes the budget materially (permits not in the budget).
- Salkantay (pass ~4,630 m) and the Santa Maria route to Machu Picchu are named by the FCDO as disrupted by rainy-season rock- and mudslides (S04). Four-day Salkantay packages are listed from roughly USD 450–860 (S17). Lares (pass ~4,400 m) is a lower-risk alternative.
- None of these is in the base plan: the 3 March-window days are used for Titicaca and Colca instead. It is a deliberate choice, not a source finding.

## Machu Picchu ticket rules

From the Andina report of the 2026 rules (S10): daily cap 4,500, rising to 5,600 on high-season dates (for 2026: 1 Jan, 2–5 Apr, 19 Jun–2 Nov, 30–31 Dec); three circuits (Panoramic, Classic, Royalty) plus mountain routes; tickets only at tuboleto.cultura.pe; valid ID or passport. National-tier prices S/64 (circuits) and S/112 (mountains); the foreign-adult price was not stated and the agency figure of ~S/152 is used in the budget as an estimate. The 2027 rules and any guide requirement must be re-checked on the official platform. March is outside the high-season dates seen for 2026.

## Transport and driving legality

- Self-drive not recommended: poor driving standards, mountain roads, landslides, blockades (S04). Two of the four have car licences; nobody rides motorcycles unlicensed (legal alternatives: buses, hired drivers, taxis).
- Trains: PeruRail/Inca Rail; PeruRail page shows 2027 bookings open (S16); fares not stated, so estimated.
- Buses: daytime only; Cruz del Sur and tourist bus operators.
- Domestic flights LIM–AQP and CUZ–LIM: LATAM, Sky, JetSMART (airlines assumed; prices not observed).
- Titicaca: community boats.

Classification: **eligible**. The route stays out of every red/orange zone and the FCDO areas. The residual risk is operational: strikes and blockades. Evidence I found: after the disputed June 2026 presidential run-off, protests were reported in Cusco and Puno and blockades were announced at Ilave (Puno) (La República, other outlets seen only in search results; S19). I could not establish the situation in November 2026. If unrest is ongoing at booking time, the status should be moved to **conditional**.

Other findings: the Aerodiana Nazca flight crash (1 August 2026, 13 dead; S13, article body not opened) is why overflights are excluded; the FCDO notes recent Ubinas volcano gas and ash emissions (S04) near Arequipa; the 2022–23 Cusco/Puno blockades are known from background knowledge and the FCDO's list of protest regions, not from a fetched report.
