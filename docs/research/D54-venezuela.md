# D54 — Venezuela: Tepuis, Tea-Coloured Rivers and the World's Tallest Waterfall

Researched 2026-11-28. Origin: participant proposal (2026-11-28). Classification: **excluded**. Concept note, not an operational plan.

## Overview

The concept: fly to Caracas, then Santa Elena de Uairén (Bolívar state), cross the Gran Sabana by 4x4 (Kamá, Quebrada de Jaspe) to Paraitepui and trek Roraima (6-8 days, compulsory Pemón guides), then fly to Canaima for Sapo Falls and Angel Falls (979 m). Optional add-ons: Mérida (Mukumbarí cable car), Los Roques, Médanos de Coro. About 14 days including two flight days.

## February vs March

February: driest, clearest weather; Carnival 8-9 Feb 2027 (domestic holiday). March: lowest water, Angel Falls thinnest, Semana Santa about 21-28 March fills flights. February preferred. Roraima summit stays cold and wet in both. Climate figures are planning normals, not forecasts. Ramadan and Lunar New Year: not relevant.

## Itinerary rationale (concept only)

No day-by-day plan is given. Sequence: Caracas, flight to Santa Elena, 1 day Gran Sabana stops, 6-8 days Roraima, flight to Canaima, 2 nights, flight to Caracas. Coordinates are approximate for hiking points.

## Transport and driving legality

Canaima has no road. Domestic tickets are often bought in cash at an office. Self-drive is not feasible. A 4x4 with driver for Santa Elena and Gran Sabana. Two of the four hold car licences, none would drive; no motorcycle riding without a licence.

## Angel Falls in the dry season

Sources agree on direction but not severity: boat trips (about 5 h by curiara plus a walk) are shortened or cancelled late in the dry season (December-May, worst around March), the falls can thin to a veil, and one operator offers a US$100 refund if drought blocks river access. The overflight (US$100-180 by operator listings) is the dependable fallback; none of these figures is confirmed for 2027.

## Security assessment

Checked 2026-11-28:

- **UK FCDO** (updated 18 Nov 2026): against all travel to the Orinoco Mining Arc in Bolívar including all of Sifontes municipality (Santa Elena de Uairén), Piar, Roscio, Cedeño, El Callo and Heres, parts of Sucre and Raúl Leoni. Against all but essential travel to parts of the country. One fetch of the main page also reported against all travel within 40 km of the Brazilian border; the regional-risks page I read did not list it. Canaima and Paraitepui were not named in what I read. Travelling against advice may invalidate insurance.
- **Netherlands** (modified 4 Sep 2026): southern Bolívar including Canaima and Roraima orange; northern Bolívar and the Brazilian border strip red. Arbitrary arrests still occur; armed-gang kidnappings in the red border areas.
- **US State Department**: Level 3 overall (27 Jun 2026) with rural Bolívar Do Not Travel; read through a search summary (HTTP 403).
- **France Diplomatie** (updated 4 Nov 2026): the fetched text had no colour code or zone detail; level for Bolívar not confirmed. Advice: carry passport copies, avoid political gatherings.
- **Smartraveller**: fetch timed out, not verified. **Canada**: avoid all travel, via search summary only.
- **Context**: US strikes and capture of Maduro on 3 January 2026; Delcy Rodríguez acting president since 5 January; political prisoners released, relations resumed in February; earthquakes on 24 June damaged Caracas and the airport, which reopened on 1 September at limited capacity.

Classification: **excluded**, because the Roraima gateway (Santa Elena, Sifontes) is in an area under advice against all travel, and Canaima and the Gran Sabana are rated orange (necessary travel only) or Do Not Travel (US). Not softened. The expedition's identity cannot be preserved by avoiding Bolívar.
