# D42 — Lebanon and Syria: Cedars, Crusader Castles and the Road to Damascus

Researched 2026-11-28. Origin: participant proposal. Classification: **excluded**. This is a concept note, not an operational plan.

## Overview

An overland Levantine concept of about 14 days: Beirut, Byblos, Jeita Grotto, the Qadisha Valley and Cedars of God (Bsharri), Baalbek in the Bekaa, across the Masnaa crossing to Damascus, Maaloula, Hama, Krak des Chevaliers, Aleppo, Palmyra and Bosra, with Sidon and Tyre as a southern option. Hired drivers and guides throughout; camping is not realistic.

## February vs March

February is cold and wet (Beirut about 14-18 C by day, Damascus 0-3 C at night, cedars under snow) and Ramadan runs from about 8 February; most restaurants in Syria close by day. March is milder (Beirut 16-22 C), the cedar road clears later in the month and Ramadan ends about 9 March; Eid brings domestic travel peaks. Figures are rounded climate normals from general knowledge; no meteorological page was fetched, so they are labelled planning values. Prefer late March.

## Itinerary rationale (concept only)

Lebanon first for the gentlest introduction (Beirut, Byblos, Jeita), the Qadisha/cedars walking, then Baalbek and the Masnaa road (about 61 km from Beirut, 55 km to Damascus) to Damascus. The Syrian loop follows the north-south axis Damascus, Hama, Krak, Aleppo, with Palmyra and Bosra as add-ons. None of this is presented as day-by-day because the route cannot be recommended.

## Transport and driving legality

Self-drive is not recommended. Cars rented in Lebanon generally cannot be taken to Syria, and Dutch advice reports carjacking of rental cars in the Bekaa. Two of the four hold car licences; no motorcycles. A hired driver with vehicle (Lebanese, then Syrian) is the only realistic model. The Masnaa crossing closed and reopened during the 2026 war (L'Orient Today); its current status was not confirmed for November 2026.

## Syria entry and tourism

- Visa: third-party guides report visa on arrival for EU citizens at about USD 75 (cash); the official e-visa portal showed only "coming soon" in September 2026. Unverified by an official Syrian source.
- On 12 November 2026 Syria briefly required pre-approval for US citizens, then cancelled it on 13 November; a notice that tourists of all nationalities must travel through an agency or operator was posted and its status is unclear (Al-Monitor). European visitors could still get visas on arrival.
- Tourism is returning: Syria reported 3.52 million visitors in H1 2026, many of them returning Syrians; operators and hotels are reopening but infrastructure is thin (The National). Palmyra, Aleppo and Bosra tour programmes could not be verified.
- Foreign tourists do travel there now, with operators. That does not change official advice.

## Budget workings (indicative, EUR per person; group of 4)

Realistic local subtotal: baggage 60 + public transport 40 + driver (11 days x 100 / 4 = 275) + hotels (2 rooms x 12 nights = 24 room-nights x 50 / 4 = 300) + food (13 x 26 = 338) + water (13 x 2 = 26) + sites 90 + activities 60 + guides (6 days x 50 / 4 = 75) + visa 75 = 1,339. Contingency 15% = 201. Flights 520. **Total 2,060** (Exceptional expenditure band). Economical 1,253; comfortable 3,095. All figures are planning estimates with no quotes.

Insurance and Syrian permits are unknown (null), not zero, so every total understates the real cost. Standard policies generally exclude countries under "against all travel" advice and war risk (Insurance Business; Canadian and US insurer notices list Lebanon and Syria as restricted). A specialist policy with evacuation and kidnap cover may exist but no price was found; it would plausibly add several hundred euros per person, which is a guess, not a source.

Findings: Israel-Hezbollah fighting escalated from 2 March 2026; a cessation of hostilities was announced in June but strikes continue, mainly in the south, Nabatieh, Beqaa and Beirut's southern suburbs. Syria has terrorism, kidnapping, unexploded ordnance and arbitrary detention risks, and no Western consular presence.

**Classification: excluded.** The Syrian half is covered by advice against all travel (UK), red (NL), Level 4 (US) and discouraged (France). The Lebanese half includes Baalbek (FCDO all but essential), the airport road (FCDO all travel) and the South, and the whole country is red in the Netherlands and Level 4 in the US. This cannot be redesigned without destroying the identity of the concept.

## Lebanon-only variant (not the destination's own route)

Hypothetical: Beirut centre, Byblos, Jeita Grotto, the Qadisha Valley and the Cedars of God, perhaps 8-10 days, no Baalbek, Sidon, Tyre or the Bekaa. The FCDO list (August 2026) does not name Byblos, Jeita, Bsharri or the Qadisha among restricted areas, and does not name central Beirut apart from the southern suburbs and the Route 51 airport road, so a stay north of the capital falls outside its restricted zones, but the airport road itself is "against all travel", which complicates arrival. France still discourages travel across most of Lebanon, the Netherlands is red for the whole country and the US is Level 4. Under the brief's rule (any red or Level 4 exposure, plus significant conflict risk) that variant would still classify as **excluded**, not conditional, as long as the Netherlands is red nationwide. It would become worth re-examining as conditional only if the Dutch and French advice eased and the ceasefire held. Rough budget would be similar to the Lebanon part above (flights about 520 plus perhaps EUR 700-900 local), and insurance would face the same problem.
