# D41 — India (Ladakh): research notes

Added at the group's request (origin: participant-proposal, 2026-11-28). Checked 2026-11-28. Security status: **conditional**.

## Overview
Concept: a 15-day winter expedition in the Union Territory of Ladakh. Fly Paris–Delhi–Leh, acclimatise for three days, attend the Stok Guru Tsechu (15–16 Feb 2027) and the Matho Nagrang (about 19–20 Feb), track snow leopards from village homestays in Rumbak (Hemis National Park) and Ulley, and finish with a western loop through Likir, Alchi, Lamayuru and Wanla. It deliberately leaves out the classic summer circuit (Nubra, Pangong, Thiksey–Hemis–Shey monasteries as a day-trip checklist) and the Chadar.

## Screening verdict
- **Seasonality:** February is the coldest month: Leh mean max 4.2 °C, mean min −9.2 °C (S09); villages at 3,900 m reach −15 to −25 °C. The Zoji La and Manali roads are closed, so Leh is reached only by air (S01, S15).
- **Flights:** Leh flights leave in the morning and are weather-dependent: IndiGo reported a temporary suspension of Leh operations after snowfall on 5 January 2026 (search-result summary; page blocked). The plan therefore has a Delhi night before Leh and two buffer days at the end.
- **Local costs:** low. Homestay and taxi rates were not obtained from providers; they are estimates.
- **Security:** conditional, see below.
- **Cultural interest:** two oracle festivals on the Tibetan lunar calendar, Alchi's 11th-century paintings, monasteries with no crowds.
- **Adventure:** strenuous because of cold and altitude rather than technique.
- **Independent travel:** self-drive and motorbike are not feasible (union taxi rules, icy roads, only two car licences, none for motorcycles).

## February vs March
February: festivals (Stok 15–16 Feb, Matho 19–20 Feb), peak snow leopard season, but coldest and most weather-exposed. March: Leh daytime near 10 °C and night −4 °C (S09), festivals over (Dosmochey 4–5 Feb also before), thaw makes village tracks muddy. Preferred window: 11–25 February 2027. Ramadan (about 8 Feb–9 Mar) affects only some Muslim-run cafés in Leh. Tibetan Losar is derived as about 7 Feb from the Tsechu date (Tsechu is the 9th day after the new year, S12); Ladakhi Losar is celebrated earlier (28 Dec reported).

## Itinerary rationale
Arrival Leh on day 3 is deliberate: the Leh district administration requires at least 48 hours of acclimatisation before going to higher areas (S06; also IIAP, S07). Day 5 (Mon 15 Feb) then meets the 48-hour mark and the first day of the Stok Tsechu. Rumbak (about 3,900 m) is reached on foot from the Zingchen road end; the plan uses three nights and two tracking days, with day 8 as the contingency. After the Matho Nagrang the group goes to Ulley (second site) and returns via Likir, Alchi and Lamayuru, then Wanla and Leh. Days 13–14 absorb flight cancellations.

**Not in the plan, and why**
- **Chadar (frozen Zanskar).** The 2026 trek was suspended because the ice did not form; officials said a continuous solid stretch of 30–35 km is needed, road-construction debris and a Leh winter that was the warmest in eight years were blamed (S10). The usual season is early January to February, so our window is the tail of it. No 2027 order, registration or medical-clearance rules could be read. Verdict: not credible to plan; extremely risky (ice failure, cold, remote evacuation); treat as an optional extra only if the district disaster authority and ALTOA announce it, and then only on a guided departure well before mid-February.
- **Pangong Tso, Nubra via Khardung La, Hanle.** All require Protected Area Permits (arranged by a registered Leh agent, groups of two or more; fee and validity not verified), lie near the Line of Actual Control or LoC, and fall in the Dutch orange "India–China border area" (S04). Winter access is road-condition dependent (sources conflict), and the Nubra–Pangong link road is reported closed in winter. Excluded to keep the route clear of the orange zone. Hanle (4,250 m, dark sky reserve) is a separate trip.
- **Ice hockey.** The Ladakh Ice Hockey League season 3 ran 29 Jan–14 Feb 2026 at NDS Stadium, Leh; a 24x7 rink opened in August 2026 (search summaries). The 2027 schedule is unknown, so a match on day 4 is an optional bonus.

## Transport and driving legality
Flight Delhi–Leh (IndiGo, Air India); in Ladakh a local union taxi with driver. Outside commercial taxis generally cannot do local sightseeing and rented self-drive cars face limits (S16; union 2015 resolution as summarised by search results). No rental, no motorbikes. IDP not needed. Satellite phones and satellite-capable GPS devices are illegal without a licence (S01, S03): do not bring an inReach.

## Security assessment (checked 2026-11-28)
- **France Diplomatie (S01, page updated 23 Nov 2026; read from the PDF export):** red for the Kashmir valley and the immediate surroundings of the LoC/Pakistan border; Srinagar–Leh road "formellement déconseillé". For Ladakh: border tensions with China, especially around the Karakoram and Pangong Tso, may affect access; avoid areas close to the border; no photography of military sites. Leh at 3,500 m: rest 24–48 h; high-altitude hikes without guide to be avoided; insurance must cover helicopter evacuation from the request.
- **UK FCDO (S02/S03, updated 7 Oct 2026, current 28 Nov 2026):** against all travel to the UT of Jammu and Kashmir and within 10 km of the India–Pakistan border; Ladakh is not listed; Manipur all-but-essential. Terrorists "very likely" to attempt attacks in India; satellite devices illegal.
- **Netherlands (S04, changed 9 Sep 2026):** red for Jammu & Kashmir, Aksai Chin, Manipur, Pakistan border strip; orange for the border area with China including Ladakh ("travel there only with a permit from the Ministry of Home Affairs"); yellow elsewhere. Ceasefire between India and Pakistan since 10 May 2025. The width of the orange area is not defined; the route avoids anything near the border.
- **Canada (S05, updated 6 Nov 2026):** high degree of caution overall; the avoid-non-essential advisory for J&K explicitly excludes Ladakh.
- **US State Department and Smartraveller:** pages could not be fetched (403 and timeout). Search summaries say the US keeps Level 4 for J&K "except the eastern Ladakh region and its capital, Leh" and Smartraveller's J&K warning does not extend to Ladakh. Not used as formal advisories; to be re-read before booking.
- **Recent events:** clashes in Leh on 24 September 2025 left at least four dead and about 100 injured; Sonam Wangchuk was detained under the National Security Act and talks with the Home Ministry remained unresolved at January 2026 (S20), with further rounds reported in 2026 (search summaries). I found no official statement that Leh was under curfew in November 2026.

**Classification: conditional.** No red or all-travel region is on the route, but the Netherlands lists Ladakh's China border area as orange, there was deadly unrest in Leh 14 months before departure with no settled outcome, and winter flights and medical access are limited. The route avoids Pangong, Nubra and Hanle. It could become eligible if Leh stays calm and the Dutch zone is clarified; it must not enter the eligible ranking meanwhile.

## Signature experiences
1. Snow leopard tracking from Rumbak and Ulley (estimated €330 pp; packages from USD 2,663 for 10 days). Rumbak homestay guests pay the household; reported rates INR 800–1,050 pp with meals (S14). Himalayan Homestays lists areas Sham, Rong and Zanskar (S19). I could not find published success rates; one operator claim of six sightings in six days is anecdotal.
2. Stok Guru Tsechu and Matho Nagrang oracle festivals (dates from two operator sites; not confirmed by an official calendar).
3. Winter homestay life at −20 °C.
4. Western Sham loop in winter.

## Alternatives considered
- **Cheaper variant:** fewer spotter and guide days, own gear, connecting flights: €1,347 (economical).
- **Safer variant:** skip the walk-in to Rumbak and track only from Ulley by road (3,800–4,000 m, less walking); fewer nights at altitude.
- **Summer Ladakh** (Nubra, Pangong, Tso Moriri): different season; outside the Feb–Mar brief.
- **Spiti or Zanskar by road in winter:** not credible for four people without winter expedition support.
