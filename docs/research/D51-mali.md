# D51 — Mali: Mud Mosques, the Niger Delta and the Dogon Cliff

Researched 2026-11-28. Origin: participant proposal. Classification: **excluded**. This is a concept note, not an operational plan.

## Overview

The classic Mali expedition of about 14 days: Bamako, Ségou (and the Kalabougou potters), Djenné (Great Mosque, UNESCO; Monday market), Mopti and a pinasse trip on the Niger inner delta, then the Bandiagara escarpment (Dogon Country, UNESCO) trekked village to village, with Timbuktu as an optional air extension. A 4x4 with driver and local guides would be the only workable format. Everything on the route lies in areas covered by official advice against all travel.

## February vs March

February is the better month: cooler nights, a fuller delta and easier trekking; the first week precedes Ramadan (about 8 February to 9 March). March is 3-4 C hotter, hazy and ends with Eid. If the route ever reopened, the first half of February would be the choice. These are climate normals, not a forecast.

## Itinerary rationale (concept only)

No day-by-day plan is given because the route is excluded. The sequence follows the traditional circuit: Bamako (1 night) to Ségou, to Djenné in time for Monday's market, to Mopti for the delta, to Bandiagara for a four- to five-day trek ending near Sangha, back by road or, optionally, a flight to Timbuktu. Coordinates for the delta and cliff villages are approximate.

## Transport and driving legality

Self-drive is not feasible: highway blockades and attacks (FCDO), fuel shortages (Netherlands, JNIM blockade), checkpoints. A 4x4 with driver would be required. Two of the four hold car licences; none would drive. No motorcycle riding without the required licence. Overland exit to neighbouring countries is judged too dangerous by the FCDO.

Context: JNIM's fuel blockade started in September 2025, eased in early 2026 and was re-imposed on Bamako on 28 April 2026 (Wikipedia compilation; current status unverified). On 25 April 2026 coordinated attacks hit Kati, the area around Bamako airport, Kidal, Gao, Sévaré and Mopti and killed the defence minister; fighting continued to at least September 2026. Sky Mali suspended flights to the centre and north. Reports describe a kidnapping increase of foreigners in 2025 (ACLED) and abuses by army and armed groups (HRW). The Dogon area and the road from Mopti are cited by travel-risk sites as among the most dangerous; those sources were not primary and are not relied on. Mali is run by a military government. No insurer covering the country was found.

**Classification: excluded.** Every stop is under advice against all travel, and kidnapping and terrorism risk are severe on the actual route. It would not be reassessed by redesign: the route's identity (Djenné, delta, Dogon) is in the worst-affected zone.

## Signature experiences (concept)

The Great Mosque and Monday market of Djenné; a pinasse through the inner delta; the Bandiagara escarpment trek; Timbuktu's mosques (optional).

## Alternatives considered

D16 Senegal (read briefly) is the accessible Sahel and river-culture alternative already in the portfolio: Saloum Delta by pirogue, Fadiouth, Lompoul dunes and Saint-Louis, with Wolof and Serer culture and a realistic total near EUR 1,800. D09 Egypt and D08 Tunisia offer earthen or desert architecture. Mauritania's Adrar and Burkina Faso are not options (similar advice). No cheaper or safer variant of Mali exists.
