# D29 — Argentina: Granite Spires, Glacier Ice and the End of the Road

Research date: 28 November 2026. Added from a participant proposal. Prices are planning figures at November 2026 levels; nothing here is a live quote.

## 1. Overview

Fourteen days, 6–19 March 2027: Buenos Aires (1 night), flight to El Calafate, Perito Moreno glacier, a 215-km drive to El Chaltén with four nights there (Laguna Torre, one camp night at Poincenot, a dawn climb to Laguna de los Tres, one spare weather day), a flight to Ushuaia for Tierra del Fuego National Park and the Beagle Channel, then two nights back in Buenos Aires. Realistic cost about €3,450 per person with flights: outside the €2,500 limit. Security: eligible.

## 3. February vs March

SMN 1991–2020 normals, El Calafate Airport: February high 19.2 °C, low 6.8 °C, 13.7 mm (3.1 days); March high 16.6 °C, low 4.7 °C, 16.7 mm (4.0 days). Ushuaia Airport: February 13.6/5.2 °C, 34.6 mm (13.3 days); March 12.2/4.6 °C, 38.3 mm (15.1 days). El Chaltén (1941–50, old series, wetter, mountain side): February 17.0/7.3 °C, 62 mm; March 14.7/4.6 °C, 67 mm.

February is the warmest and highest-demand month; mountain camps, Chaltén rooms and rental cars sell out first. March is cooler with fewer people; early snow on the high trails is a risk by late March, and penguins on Isla Martillo are leaving. Recommended window: 6–19 March 2027. Argentine Carnival falls 8–9 February 2027 (derived from Easter on 28 March 2027) and is not relevant to Patagonia. Argentina's 24 March holiday is outside the window. Ramadan and Lunar New Year are not relevant.

## 4. Itinerary rationale

Patagonia only. The 14-day length forces a choice between regions; Patagonia is the single coherent concept for February–March.

- **Torres del Paine** across the border is excluded: a one-way or cross-border rental is restricted (most Argentine rentals cannot leave the country; Fox needs 96 h notice and paperwork), the Chilean park requires reservations months ahead, and it would turn an Argentina trip into a Chile trip.
- **Ruta 40 gravel** (the long north–south route) would add days of driving on unpaved sections for no extra reward; this plan uses the paved RP11/RN40 section between Calafate and Chaltén only.
- **Ushuaia** is kept because it adds a sub-Antarctic coast and the Yámana presence to an otherwise mountain-led plan, at the price of two domestic flights.
- **Buenos Aires** bookends the trip because Air France lands there; two nights give an urban counterweight.

Day plan: see the JSON. Contingency: day 8 (flexible), and day 13 (late checkout). Weather rule: Fitz Roy and Torre days can be swapped; Poincenot camp can be moved to day 8.

## 5. Transport and driving legality

- Domestic flights: Aerolíneas Argentinas; Flybondi and JetSMART on some routes (not verified). Fare data found: Buenos Aires–Ushuaia March 2027 from ARS 325,059 return (Almundo) and a 12-month average of ARS 251,384 return (Kayak). AEP–FTE and FTE–USH fares and timetables are not verified. Direct FTE–USH flights may not run daily: check before booking.
- Rental: round trip from El Calafate (7 days). Hertz El Calafate averages roughly US$39–99 a day over a year. Minimum age 21; additional driver about US$7 a day (Fox); most cars cannot leave Argentina; excess up to US$9,000 on a total loss or rollover (National Car, per search summary).
- Licence: FCDO says a UK licence needs a 1949 IDP; the Dutch advice says Dutch licences need an IDP; Fox requires an IDP if the licence is not in English, Portuguese or Spanish; Hertz says either. Take an IDP.
- Two travellers drive; two do not. No motorcycle use. Alternatives: bus El Calafate–El Chaltén (ARS 70,000 one way, about €41), walking in Chaltén, shuttles in Ushuaia.
- Roads: RP11 and RN40 used are paved. Wind and livestock are the hazard; France Diplomatie says road violence is the main safety concern.

## 7. Security assessment

- **France Diplomatie** country page (updated 2 Nov 2026): alerts on El Niño (very strong episode, most virulent October 2026–February 2027), enhanced vigilance for French nationals abroad (10 Sep 2026) and measles (13 Mar 2026). No zone against travel appears in the fetched text; the security map is an image, so the exact map colour is **not confirmed**. Sécurité page (updated 15 Sep 2026): vigilance zones by city; road violence is the main concern; Ushuaia at highest seismic level; rental car break-ins in Bariloche and Puerto Madryn.
- **UK FCDO** (updated 7 May 2026): no warning; crime, protests, earthquakes, IDP, rescue costs, hantavirus present.
- **Nederland Wereldwijd** (updated 25 Nov 2026): yellow; Rosario gang violence; north-west flooding December–March.
- **Smartraveller** (timed out) and **US State Department** (HTTP 403) not read.

Classification: eligible. The north-west was not chosen partly for flooding and road washouts in the rainy season per the Dutch advice.

## 8. Signature experiences

Dawn at Laguna de los Tres under Fitz Roy; Perito Moreno boardwalks and the ice trek; Laguna Torre day; Beagle Channel and Tierra del Fuego. Details in the JSON.

## 9. Alternatives considered

- **North-west (Salta, Quebrada de Humahuaca, Cafayate, Cachi, Salinas Grandes):** Carnival in Humahuaca falls 8–9 February 2027, and the region is in the rainy season December–March, when the Dutch advice warns of flooding that can make roads impassable. Altitude (Salinas Grandes about 3,400 m) and earthquakes (Salta, Jujuy per FCDO) add constraints. Not recommended for February–March; better in the dry season. A February Carnival option exists but road-closure risk was not assessed in depth.
- **Patagonia + North-west by domestic flights:** needs at least 16–18 days; too long.
- **Patagonia only (12 days):** the cheaper variant above.
- **Torres del Paine:** see section 4.
