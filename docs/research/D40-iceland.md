# D40 — Iceland: Fire, Ice and the Winter South Coast

Researched 28 November 2026 for a proposal by the group. Data file: `data/destinations/D40-iceland.json`.

## Overview

A 12-day self-drive out and back along the southern half of Route 1 in early March 2027: Reykjavik, the Golden
Circle (Þingvellir, Geysir, Gullfoss), Seljalandsfoss, Skógafoss, Reynisfjara, Skaftafell, Jökulsárlón and Diamond
Beach, a guided ice cave in Vatnajökull, Höfn as the turning point, and a Reykjanes finish. Nights in guesthouses.

## February vs March

February has the best ice-cave reputation (a commercial guide recommends it) and darkest skies for aurora, but only
7 to 10 hours of light and the most frequent storm closures. March is preferred: more daylight, caves still run
(season dates vary by source; one lists Crystal Cave until 15 April, another mid-November to mid-March), aurora
remains active around the equinox. Easter week raises prices and closures. Temperatures are about 0 to 4 C by day
and -5 to 0 C at night; these are approximate ranges, the Icelandic Met Office normals were not retrieved.

## Itinerary rationale

Full Ring Road in winter was rejected: about 1,300 km of Route 1 plus the north-east (Dettifoss road closed or
unreliable in winter, Mývatn and Húsavík far from the south), with long driving days on ice and wind closures
that can strand a group for a day or more. An out-and-back to Höfn gives about 1,460 km of driving, a daily average
of about 3 h, two flexible days (8 and 11) and a way back if the road closes. Snæfellsnes was considered instead of
the glacier side: it is closer to Reykjavik and safer, but has no ice cave, glacier lagoon or Skógafoss. It stays an
alternative (below).

Days: 1 arrive and optional Blue Lagoon, 2 Reykjavik, 3 Golden Circle (Silfra optional), 4 Seljalandsfoss and
Skógafoss to Vík, 5 Reynisfjara and Kirkjubæjarklaustur, 6 Skaftafell, 7 ice cave and Jökulsárlón, 8 flexible at Höfn,
9 back to Vík, 10 Reykjadalur and Reykjavik, 11 Reykjanes and spare day, 12 fly home.

## Transport and driving legality

- Self-drive 4x4 SUV with studded tyres; F-roads are closed in winter and off-road driving is illegal.
- Two of the four travellers have car licences and are added as drivers. Many companies require age 20 or more, and
  sometimes 23 for 4x4s. Check with the company. A licence in a non-Latin alphabet needs an international permit.
- No motorcycle or scooter riding.
- Insurance: gravel, sand-and-ash, theft and super collision cover. The FCDO warns that sand and ash damage may not
  be covered by standard insurance.
- Daily checks: umferdin.is / road.is, vedur.is and safetravel.is (a wind alert of up to 35 m/s was posted for
  26–27 November 2026 between Vík and Höfn on the Safetravel homepage).

## Camping and campervans

Not recommended. Most campsites are closed in winter; wind, sleet and temperatures near 0 C make tents unsafe, and a
campervan needs winter heating and costs more than guesthouse beds. Which campsites stay open was not verified.

Classification: eligible. No conflict, terrorism or kidnapping concern. The Reykjanes stops (Blue Lagoon, Seltún,
the lighthouse) are optional and can be dropped without changing the route's identity. Keflavik airport has stayed
open during all eruptions according to the FCDO and Visit Reykjanes pages.

Reykjanes status. The Visit Reykjanes page (updated 30 August 2025) says the July–August 2025 eruption ended, Grindavík
was open and the Blue Lagoon open. A secondary source (The Icelandic Guide, 14 July 2026) reports an Icelandic Met
Office warning on 13 July 2026 that Svartsengi magma had reached thresholds seen before eruptions, and that an eruption
could start with 20 minutes to 4 hours of seismic warning. Search summaries of other travel sites also state an
orange alert level and a hazard assessment for Grindavík valid to 5 January 2027, but these were not verified on
en.vedur.is (its news page did not show Reykjanes items). Reassess before booking and before each Reykjanes day.

Reynisfjara. A warning-light system (yellow for moderate and red for high danger) and a gate operate after fatal
accidents; press reports say a child died in August 2025 while the light was yellow and the system was changed.
Page text was not read. The group stays at least 25 m from the sea at all times and never enters the wave zone.

Ice caves. After the August 2024 collapse at Breiðamerkurjökull that killed a tourist, the park contracts 30
operators (Grapevine, September 2025), limits guests to 8–16 per guide and requires daily risk assessment. The 2026–27
conditions were not retrieved from the park; operators decide daily and may cancel. Book with a contracted operator
only.

## Signature experiences

1. Blue ice cave in Vatnajökull, guided, about EUR 150.
2. Jökulsárlón and Diamond Beach at dawn and dusk.
3. Seljalandsfoss, Skógafoss and Reynisfjara with the warning lights.
4. Silfra snorkelling and the Golden Circle.
5. Aurora evenings away from Reykjavik (odds depend on cloud; no probability is given by the Met Office page).

## Alternatives considered

- Snæfellsnes instead of the east: safer and cheaper (shorter drive), but no glacier lagoon or ice cave.
- Full Ring Road: not recommended in winter for the reasons above.
- Campervan: rejected, see above.
- Langjökull snowmobiling: optional extra, outside the realistic budget.
