# D30 — Côte d'Ivoire: Beyond Abidjan (research notes)

Checked 2026-11-28. Origin: participant proposal. Security status: **conditional**.

## Overview
A 13-day loop that uses Abidjan only as a gateway: Grand-Bassam (UNESCO-listed colonial quarter) and Assinie on the lagoon coast, Yamoussoukro's basilica, four nights around Man in the Dan highlands (Dent de Man, cascades, Mont Tonkoui, liana-bridge villages, mask tradition), then back via Yamoussoukro to Grand-Lahou on the lagoon. Hired 4×4 with driver, daylight only.

## Screening verdict
- **Seasonality:** February is the driest month, with harmattan haze; March is hotter and wetter. Climate figures are approximate normals, not station data checked here.
- **Flights:** Air France shows Paris–Abidjan fares from about €647; comparison sites give March averages of €566–598. Realistic €640 is representative, not a quote.
- **Local costs:** moderate to high for West Africa (vehicle with driver, accommodation).
- **Security:** conditional (see below).
- **Cultural interest:** Grand-Bassam, basilica, Dan culture. Adventure: moderate hikes. Independent travel: not recommended without a driver.

## February vs March
February: dry, hazy, overlaps Ramadan (about 8 Feb–9 Mar). March: rains start mid-month, humid, Easter weekend 26–28 March 2027 (local peak). Preferred: 13–25 February 2027.

## Itinerary rationale
Day 1 arrive; 2 Grand-Bassam; 3 Assinie; 4 Yamoussoukro (about 340 km); 5 Man (about 450 km, 7 h); 6–8 Man (Dent de Man and cascades; liana bridges and a Dan village; Mont Tonkoui, contingency); 9 back to Yamoussoukro; 10 Grand-Lahou; 11 Grand-Lahou (contingency); 12 Abidjan and evening flight; 13 Paris. Distances and times are planning estimates. Coordinates for the Dent de Man and the villages are approximate.
Alternatives not used because of security or uncertainty: Comoé National Park (red, excluded), Taï National Park (orange, excluded), Korhogo and the Senufo north (in or near the zones of heightened Sahel risk; not assessed in detail; excluded from the route), Sassandra and San-Pédro coast (road and timings not verified; could replace Grand-Lahou).

## Transport and driving legality
Self-drive not recommended: poor driving standards, night hazards, unofficial roadblocks and armed robberies in the west (FCDO). FCDO requires a 1968 International Driving Permit or a local licence. With a driver, the two licensed members need not drive. Nobody may ride a motorcycle without a licence; mototaxis only as passengers. Domestic flights (Air Côte d'Ivoire) were not verified and are not used.

## Security assessment (checked 2026-11-28)
- **France Diplomatie** (zone assessment updated 5 Mar 2026; page header 15 Sep 2026): red along the Mali and Burkina Faso borders, northern Zanzan, eastern Savanes and Comoé National Park; orange on the Liberian border incl. Tabou, Taï, Grabo; rest yellow (vigilance renforcée) with recommendations to travel by day in convoys of at least two vehicles and avoid night driving. The French "Conseils par pays" URL returned 404; the information-par-pays page was used.
- **UK FCDO** (updated 9 Nov 2026): against all travel within 40 km of the Mali/Burkina borders, northern Zanzan and Savanes, Comoé; against all but essential travel within 20 km of the Liberian border. Terrorism attacks cannot be ruled out anywhere; armed robbery on main roads in the west.
- **Nederland Wereldwijd** (updated 11 Jun 2026): red Mali/Burkina borders, orange Liberian strip, yellow elsewhere.
- **Belgium**: advises against travel within 50 km of the northern borders, Comoé and Bouna (summary from search results only).
- **Other:** no confirmed JNIM attack inside Côte d'Ivoire in 2026 found; Kafolo 2020 and a deadly attack near the Burkina border in 2025 are earlier. WHO declared the DRC/Uganda Ebola outbreak a PHEIC on 17 May 2026; Côte d'Ivoire runs screening and a health declaration for travellers from Africa. Not fully verified for November 2026.
- **Classification:** the route avoids every red and orange zone, so not excluded. Classified **conditional** because France's yellow-zone guidance (day travel, convoy of two vehicles) is not met by the baseline and would breach the €2,500 limit if met, armed-robbery risk on western roads, and the Sahel spillover trend. Could move to eligible if official advice relaxes or the group accepts the residual risk with a driver and local operator who confirms current road conditions.
