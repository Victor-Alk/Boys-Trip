# D46 — China — Tibet: research notes

Researched 28 November 2026. Origin: group request (participant proposal, 28 November 2026). All prices are planning estimates unless stated; no agency quotation for winter 2027 was obtained.

## Overview

A 13-day guided journey: Chengdu, flight to Lhasa (3,650 m), three Lhasa days (Jokhang and Barkhor, Potala, Sera debate), drive over Kampa La (about 4,800 m) and Karo La (about 5,050 m) to Gyantse (Kumbum), Shigatse (Tashilhunpo), two nights at Shegar/New Tingri (4,300 m) with a midday visit to Rongbuk Monastery and the north-side Everest Base Camp (about 5,150 m), then back to Lhasa and Chengdu. Window: 12 to 24 February 2027. D36 (China West) deliberately excluded the Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR); D41 (Ladakh) covers Himalayan Buddhist culture without a permit regime.

## Screening verdict

**Feasibility comes first, and it is unresolved.**

- **Closure pattern.** The TAR has historically been closed to foreign tourists around Tibetan New Year and the March anniversaries. Operator-reported history: about 1 to 18 March 2024 (permits resumed 19 March), about 1 to 19 March 2025 (plus an Everest-area closure after the 7 January 2025 earthquake until 7 March), February 2019 closure from about 1 February to 1 March (S07, S08, S09). For 2026 two operators report no March closure and the TAR open (Lhasa, Shigatse, Everest all open as of 22 October 2026), but this is agency reporting, not an official Chinese notice (S07, S08). Nothing is known for 2027, and notices usually come only a few weeks before. No Tibet Tourism Bureau notice could be found.
- **Conclusion.** March 2027 is rated poor (closure in two of the last three years, and the dates are not announced). February is rated fair: open in 2024 and reportedly in 2025 and 2026, but not guaranteed. The permit is only issued 2 to 4 weeks before travel, so the group would hold non-refundable flights without knowing if the trip can happen. Mitigation: book refundable or flexible Paris to Chengdu fares and book the agency only on a refundable basis; Chengdu day 2 and Lhasa day 12 are buffers.
- **Early-February window before the closure?** Yes in principle: the recent closures began about 1 March, so February is the only month with a record of access. Starting on 12 February avoids the Spring Festival holiday peak (5 to 13 February, S20) and follows Losar (7 February, S21). Going earlier (the week of Losar itself) means holiday prices, closed shops and a heavier security presence in Lhasa.
- **Other entry conditions.** Visa-free entry for French citizens ends 31 December 2026, no extension announced as of 28 November 2026 (S18, S19, S03). The Tibet Travel Permit is arranged only by a licensed agency (2 to 3 weeks, 3 to 4 weeks around Tibetan New Year, S10; 15 to 20 working days advised in March 2025, S23) and is checked at check-in for Lhasa flights and trains. An Aliens' Travel Permit is needed for Shigatse, Gyantse and the Everest region (S08, S10). A guide is compulsory throughout; itinerary changes on the ground are not allowed (S10).
- **Cultural interest:** very high. **Adventure:** moderate to strenuous because of altitude and cold, not technique. **Independent-travel feasibility:** none; this is the least independent destination in the portfolio.

## Itinerary rationale

- Acclimatisation: three nights in Lhasa (3,650 m); Gyantse (3,980 m), Shigatse (3,840 m), Shegar (4,300 m, two nights); Base Camp (5,150 m) as a day trip so nobody sleeps higher than 4,300 m. The Rongbuk guesthouse (4,980 m) is not recommended for the first Everest visit.
- The Potala is closed on Mondays (S13), so the Jokhang and Barkhor are on Monday 15 February and the Potala on Tuesday 16.
- Days 2 and 12 are flexible: a late permit, flight delay, a monastery closure or a bad-weather loss can be absorbed.
- The Qinghai–Tibet railway from Xining (20 to 21.5 h, hard sleeper CNY 521, S14) was considered and rejected as the main option because it needs a Chengdu to Xining leg and adds about two days; it remains a fallback if flights are full during Spring Festival.
- Namtso (frozen and snow-blocked), Ngari/Kailash and Nyingchi are not in the plan.

## Transport and driving legality

Foreign visitors cannot drive or rent vehicles in the TAR: all road travel is by agency van with driver and a guide (S09, S10). Motorcycles are not available to foreigners. The two licence-holders gain nothing from their licences on this trip. Chengdu to Lhasa by air is about 2 h 40 min and one-way fares are about USD 220 to 330 (S10). Oxygen is supplied in the vehicle (S09).

**Classification: conditional.** No advisory forbids the route and the risk of conflict, terrorism or kidnapping is low. The route restriction (possible closure and permit refusal, no 2027 information) is an unresolved route restriction under the brief. Insurance cover is also doubtful until a policy is confirmed (S17). The Nepal border floods affect Gyirong, not the Shigatse to Everest road, but road damage elsewhere could cause detours.

## Alternatives considered

- Xining to Lhasa by train: extra two days, cheaper in transport, same permit gate.
- Entry from Nepal (Kodari/Kyirong): both crossings are closed or restricted after the August 2026 floods (S07); also needs a China Group Visa.
- A Tibetan-areas-outside-the-TAR trip (Sichuan, Qinghai, Gansu): already covered by D36, with fewer permit constraints.
- Ladakh (D41) for a similar Himalayan Buddhist culture, though February access there is limited.
