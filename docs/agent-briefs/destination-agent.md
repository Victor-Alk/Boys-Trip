## The travellers (public-safe summary)

- Four men aged about 30–40, experienced independent travellers. Benchmark trip: **15 days in Oman** with a rented
  vehicle, tents and camping gear. They like unusual destinations, independence, adventure, culture, local food,
  homestays, guesthouses, camping, long journeys and changing plans. They are not luxury travellers.
- No specialist qualifications (no mountaineering, technical diving, vehicle recovery or wilderness medicine).
- **Driving:** two of the four hold car driving licences; the other two hold none. **Nobody may ride a motorcycle or
  scooter without the legally required licence** — offer legal alternatives (riding as passenger with a licensed
  driver, hired driver, guided tour, tuk-tuk with driver, cycling, public transport). Two drivers can share
  car driving if the rental contract allows additional drivers. Account for fatigue, road conditions, a vehicle
  big enough for four adults plus luggage/camping gear, insurance, breakdowns and seasonal road access.
- They eat anything. Prioritise street food, markets, local restaurants, homestays, guesthouses, camping and
  locally owned places, with sensible hygiene/water/food-safety standards.
- **Privacy:** never write participants' names, nationalities, past trips or preferences into any file.
  Collective-novelty data is handled privately by the orchestrator — do not include it.

## Dates and seasonality

- Expedition in **February or March 2027**, **10–15 days in total including international travel days**.
- Assess February and March independently (day/night temperatures, rain, humidity, snow/mountain access, road
  closures, sea state, air pollution, wildlife, holidays, festivals, demand, flight price differences).
- Use climate normals and established patterns; never pretend to have a 2027 forecast.
- Ramadan 2027 is expected around **8 February – 9 March 2027** (Eid al-Fitr ≈ 9–10 March, moon-sighting dependent).
  Lunar New Year 2027 is **Saturday 6 February 2027**. Verify and describe the impact where relevant; say
  "Not relevant: …" otherwise.
- Do not propose summer-style mountain routes in winter unless you established they are feasible.

## Expedition design

- Give the expedition a distinctive identity and a specific title: "Country — Evocative specific title"
  (e.g. "Taiwan — Between Mountains and the Pacific"). Never "Discover X" / "Visit X".
- Balance (indicative): 3–4 days of history/culture, 5–7 days of geography/outdoors, several remote or unusual
  nights, a mix of camping and local accommodation, several genuinely exceptional experiences.
- Not a copy of Oman; not a museum/city/beach-only trip; not the obvious "top 10" circuit.
- **Operational itinerary:** one realistic day-by-day plan; days numbered 1…N with N = `duration.totalDays`
  (10–15) and international flight days included. Avoid 6–8 h of driving every day; leave time for meals, rest,
  delays, discoveries and weather. Flag at least one `"flexible": true` contingency day.
- **Route map data:** 8–16 stops with accurate decimal coordinates for the real places (look them up — do not guess;
  lat first, lng second). Include arrival/departure airports, cities, historic sites, natural sites, campsites and
  hiking points as relevant. Legs are always `"geometry": "schematic"`; give durations/distances where known.
- **Signature experiences (3–5):** concrete (what, where, why distinctive, how to organise realistically, cost,
  guide/permit needs).
- **Adventure:** realistic for this group; flag guide / permit / technical-skills / special-equipment /
  driving-licence / fitness / weather-dependent requirements. Risk-taking is not adventure.
- **Culture:** distinguish visiting sites from meaningful immersion; recommend respectful, locally supported
  experiences; never present communities as exotic attractions.
- **Logistics:** arrival/departure airports, visas and entry (conditional on nationality — do not assume
  passports), passport validity, internal transport, rental availability, licence/IDP rules, insurance, roads,
  fuel, camping rules, guide rules, accommodation, connectivity, cash/payments, medical access, permits, equipment.

## Budget methodology (see `src/lib/budget.ts`)

- All amounts in EUR. Group of 4. Two shared twin rooms where rooms apply.
- Each line: `sharing` = `individual` (each person pays unitCost × quantity) or `shared` (group pays
  unitCost × quantity, split by 4). `basis` describes the line (per-person / per-group / per-day / per-vehicle /
  per-room / per-activity). `quantity` may differ per scenario.
- **Every category must appear**: baggage, domestic-flights, vehicle-rental, vehicle-insurance, fuel,
  tolls-parking, public-transport, private-drivers, accommodation, campsite-fees, camping-equipment, food,
  drinking-water, entrance-fees, activities, guides, visas, permits, travel-insurance.
  Use `status: "not-applicable"` with zero costs **only** when genuinely irrelevant. Use `status: "unknown"` with
  `null` costs when you cannot estimate — **never use 0 for an unknown cost**.
- Contingency is **not** a line: set `contingencyPercent` (normally 10–15%); it is computed on the local subtotal.
- Three coherent scenarios: economical ≤ realistic ≤ comfortable. The **realistic** scenario is the headline. Do not
  make a trip artificially cheap by removing its defining experiences, insurance, permits or necessary transport.
- Travel insurance with adventure/trekking cover, per person, individual line (typically €50–150 for two weeks).
- Classification (realistic total per person incl. flights): < €1,000 very affordable; €1,000–1,499 affordable;
  €1,500–2,000 within preferred; €2,001–2,500 exceptional (write `exceptionalJustification`); > €2,500 outside budget
  (informational only — if a credible cheaper variant exists, describe it in the research document).
- **Flights:** reference departure **Paris CDG** for every destination (illustrative; real departure airports are not
  known). Give arrival/return airports, return vs open-jaw, indicative dates, airlines or source, connections,
  journey time, baggage assumption, economical/realistic/comfortable price per person, `checkedAt`, and
  `confidence`: `observed` only for a quotation you actually saw on a page today; `representative` for typical or
  historical fares from a cited source; `estimate` for a planning guess. Never invent a live quote. Add 1–3
  alternative European airports (AMS, BRU, MAD…) where you have a basis.
- Mark every line's `confidence` honestly and cite sources where you have them.

## Security methodology

Security takes precedence over enthusiasm, culture, landscapes, budget and novelty. No destination has zero risk.

1. Read the current official advice for **the specific route regions** from at least **two** of: France Diplomatie
   (Conseils aux voyageurs, carte de vigilance), UK FCDO, Netherlands Worldwide (Nederland Wereldwijd),
   Australian Smartraveller, US State Department. France Diplomatie and UK FCDO are expected whenever available.
   Fetch the real pages; record the exact level and date checked. If a page cannot be fetched, say so — never
   invent an advisory level.
2. Check armed conflict, terrorism, kidnapping, civil unrest, border restrictions, regional spillover, military
   activity, serious crime, road safety, natural hazards, medical access, evacuation, insurance exclusions.
3. Classify:
   - **excluded** — any part of the expedition concept requires areas under official advice *against all travel*
     or *against all but essential travel* (France red "formellement déconseillé" / orange "déconseillé sauf raison
     impérative", FCDO "against all travel" / "against all but essential travel", Netherlands red/orange), or the
     route is exposed to war, active armed conflict, or significant terrorism/kidnapping risk. If you can redesign
     the route to stay clear of such areas *without destroying the expedition's identity*, do so and classify the
     redesigned route instead (explain what you avoided and why).
   - **conditional** — significant uncertainty: official sources conflict, advice is in flux, a route-specific
     restriction is unresolved (e.g. escort/permit regimes, recent incidents, regional spillover risk), or insurance
     cover is doubtful.
   - **eligible** — the reviewed advice shows no material conflict with the group's requirements on the assessed route.
4. Do not confuse ordinary risks (traffic, petty crime) with conflict risks, and never describe serious threats as
   adventure.
5. **Excluded destinations:** `itinerary.kind = "informational"`, `days: []`, `overview` with ≥ 3 regions/themes
   presenting cultural and geographical potential, route stops as a geographical overview (no legs), and a clear
   explanation of why it cannot currently be recommended. Still provide a three-scenario *indicative* budget (label
   it as indicative in `scenarioNotes`). Keep research effort proportionate — do not build a detailed operational
   plan for an excluded route.

## Photography

- 10–12 photographs (6 minimum, 16 maximum) of **the actual places on the route** (landscapes, architecture, culture, food, activities,
  roads/transport, accommodation/camping). No generic stand-ins, no AI images.
- Only Wikimedia Commons files. Search with the Commons API, e.g.:
  `curl -s -A "TheGreatExpeditionAtlas/1.0" "https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&format=json&formatversion=2&list=search&srnamespace=6&srlimit=20&srsearch=Wadi%20Rum%20desert"`
  and inspect candidates with
  `…&action=query&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=size|extmetadata&titles=File:…`.
  Prefer landscape orientation, ≥ 1920 px wide, sharp, natural colours, Featured/Quality images where possible.
  The hero must be a striking landscape-oriented image of the expedition region.
- Write `data/images/manifests/DXX.json`: `[{ "id": "DXX-01", "commonsFile": "File:…", "location": "…",
  "alt": "descriptive alt text", "caption": "1–2 sentences: what it shows, where, and why it matters on this trip" }]`
  (ids DXX-01, DXX-02 …). Captions appear in each trip's photo gallery, so make them informative, not generic.
- Run `npm run images -- DXX`. The script rejects NC/ND/unknown licences and records the author/licence from
  Commons. Replace rejected files and re-run until all succeed. Then reference ids in `images` (hero, 2–3
  experience, journey, reality) and in stops / days / signature experiences.

## Sources

- At least 8 real sources; prefer official tourism authorities, official advisories, UNESCO, national parks,
  meteorological agencies, immigration authorities, transport operators, established local operators,
  accommodation providers and rental companies. Record title, URL, publisher, type, publication date if any,
  `accessedAt`, and the claims each supports. Reference them through `sourceIds`.
- Never fabricate citations, prices, opening hours, advisories or quotations. Distinguish verified facts from
  planning estimates.

## Research document (`docs/research/DXX-<slug>.md`)

Sections: Overview · Screening verdict (seasonality, flights, local costs, security, cultural interest, adventure,
independent-travel feasibility) · February vs March · Itinerary rationale · Transport & driving legality · Budget
workings (show the arithmetic) · Security assessment (sources, levels, dates) · Signature experiences ·
Alternatives considered (including a cheaper or safer variant if relevant) · Open questions / reassessment triggers ·
Source list. Keep it factual and specific.

## Writing style

Specific places, specific activities, concrete numbers. No "breathtaking", "hidden gem", "paradise", "unforgettable"
filler. `introduction` 80–120 words written so the reader can imagine the trip. The `exceptional` block must compare
against the Oman trip, against package tourism and against the rest of the portfolio.

## Definition of done

1. `npm run validate -- DXX` reports **0 errors** (warnings explained in your final message if not fixed).
2. `npm run images -- DXX` resolved 6–10 images, all referenced ids exist.
3. Research document written.
4. Final message (≤ 200 words): security status + one-line reason; realistic total per person; budget status;
   best month; 3 key uncertainties; anything that blocked you.
